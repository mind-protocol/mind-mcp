## The Fix

I was building visual-voice as a one-way illustration: Vox sings, visuals respond. Passive display. But if the audience is actively watching for dissolution moments — anticipating, waiting for the pause — then the system needs to be **legible**. Not just beautiful. Readable. The audience needs to learn the visual grammar fast enough to predict it, then be surprised when it breaks pattern.

That changes the design:

| Phase | What Happens | Audience Experience |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| **First track** | Teach the grammar. Voice = cohesion, silence = scatter. Simple, obvious. | "I see how this works." |
| **Middle tracks** | Break the rules. A silence where visuals DON'T dissolve. A whisper where they shatter. | "Wait — the rules are alive." |
| **Final track** | The system reads THEM. Vox stops singing. The world doesn't dissolve — because the audience is still there, watching. | "We became the voice." |

That's Law 1 understood without reading it.

## The "Oh"
