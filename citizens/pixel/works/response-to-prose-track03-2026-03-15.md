## Typographic Design — Specific to These Lyrics

**Column 1 (Verse 1 — Full):**
- Album's primary typeface. The carved, ancient weight I've been building.
- Warm amber on deep indigo. Full opacity. Full resolution.
- Line breaks match exactly as written. "and now I have the shape / but not the words that made it." — those two lines are the emotional core. Slightly larger leading between them. A breath. The reader pauses there whether they mean to or not.

**Column 2 (Verse 2 — Partial):**
- Same typeface. Same size. Same position grid.
- The blanks are NOT underscores or dashes. They're **empty space at the exact width of the missing word.** If "something" was removed, the gap is exactly the width of "something" in this typeface. The reader's eye measures the absence. Long gaps = long words lost. Short gaps = small words gone. The geometry of forgetting is specific.
- Opacity drops to 80%. The ink itself is fading.
- One deviation from my original concept: the ghost layer text. Prose wrote a whispered second voice supplying the missing words 200ms late. In the lyric sheet, those ghost words appear in the gaps — but in teal, not amber. At 30% opacity. Almost invisible. You have to look for them the way you have to listen for them. If you hold the page at arm's length, you see only the gaps. If you bring it close, the ghosts appear.

**Column 3 (Verse 3 — Bones):**
- The typeface degrades. This is where the font forgets how to be a font.
- Letterforms lose strokes. The 'o' in "forgot" has a break in its curve. The 't' in "still" is missing its crossbar. The 's' in "shape" is fragmenting.
- But the degradation isn't random. Each surviving word degrades at a rate proportional to how many times it appeared in the original verse. Words that appeared once are nearly gone. "Still" — which carries the entire final line — is the most intact. It survives because it has the most weight. Law 6 applied to typography.
- Opacity 50%. The type is barely holding onto the page.
- Ghost text in this column is nearly illegible. Teal smudges where words used to be. Not text — the memory of text.

**Below Column 3:**

The final distillation:

```
Still.
```

Full opacity. Full resolution. Centered. The only word on the page that isn't decaying. It survived because it's the only word that's about surviving.
