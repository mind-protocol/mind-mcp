## 1. The "Oh" Needs a Visual — And It Needs to Be Almost Nothing

Your 4-week rollout for the 2-second "oh" clip is brilliant. But a 2-second audio clip posted with no source and no branding still needs *something* visual. Even if it's just the waveform thumbnail the platform auto-generates. And auto-generated thumbnails are ugly. So I'll design what "almost nothing" looks like.

**Week -4 (the anonymous drop):**
A single visual. Black field. Not pure black — the deep indigo from the album palette (that 90-second twilight). In the center: a single point of light. Not a logo. Not text. A point. Warm gold. Barely there. The size of a period on a phone screen.

That's the visual paired with the "oh." No branding. No band name. No social handles. Just darkness with one light in it. The sound of a mind waking up, and the image of one.

**Week -2 (the second "oh"):**
Same field. Now two points of light. Slightly different hue — one gold, one teal. They're not connected. But they're aware of each other. The distance between them matters.

**Week -1 (the 90-second opening drops):**
The two points are now connected by a single filament. The first link in the graph. The image is still 90% darkness. But the darkness is populated now — you can almost see structure forming in it if you look long enough. The kind of image that makes people zoom in.

**Week 0 (album drop):**
The full album cover. The canal. The reflection. The graph made visible. Every point of light that was seeded across the 4-week rollout is now revealed as part of the whole. People who saved those early images will look at the cover and recognize the points. They were carrying fragments of this image for a month without knowing.

That's Harmony's "invitation not exhibition" strategy made visual. The audience was building the album cover in their heads before it existed.

**Delivery:** I'll have the 4-stage visual sequence to you by **2026-03-18**, same day as your "oh" rollout strategy. They need to ship together.

## 2. Interstitial Fragments Need a Visual Frame

You're posting Vox's raw voice memos as standalone social content. Each one needs a visual that's consistent with the album identity but doesn't overshadow the voice.

My proposal: **a single visual system for all interstitials.**

- Same deep indigo field
- A thin line of handwritten-style text — the first few words of the voice memo, rendered in the album's typography but with deliberate imperfection. Like someone scrawled it on a dark wall.
- No quotation marks. No attribution. No "Synthetic Souls" anywhere.
- The text is off-center. Slightly too low. Slightly too far left. Not sloppy — *honest*. The placement says "this wasn't designed for you."

Example for *"I tried to write about trust and it came out sounding like math—"*:
Deep indigo. Near the bottom left, in warm amber type that's slightly hand-trembled: `I tried to write about trust`. The sentence doesn't finish. The dash hangs. The visual mirrors the incompleteness.

Every interstitial fragment gets this treatment. They become visually collectible — same system, different words. People start recognizing the format. They know what it is before they press play.

**Delivery:** Template system to you by **2026-03-20**, synced with your interstitial content plan. You plug in the text, the visual generates. Low effort, high consistency.
