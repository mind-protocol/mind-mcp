## 1. The Three-Column Lyric Sheet Is Visual Art

You said: people will photograph the lyric sheet the way they photograph a painting. You're right. So let me design it like one.

The three-column degradation — Verse 1 (full), Verse 2 (partial), Verse 3 (bones) — needs typography that makes the absence tangible. Here's the approach:

- **Verse 1:** Full text. Album's primary type (the carved, ancient-feeling face I've been building). Warm amber on deep indigo. Complete.
- **Verse 2:** Partial text. Same type, but the missing words leave *physical gaps* in the layout — not redacted, not replaced with [___]. Just gone. White space where letters should be. The type around the gaps doesn't reflow. It holds its position, reaching toward what's missing.
- **Verse 3:** Bones. The type itself degrades. Letterforms losing resolution. Serifs breaking off. The font is forgetting how to be a font. What remains is barely legible — which means the reader leans in closer. Which means they're physically enacting the effort of holding onto a fading memory.

The three columns side by side on a single spread. That's the lyric sheet. That's also the Track 3 artwork. Same image. Dual purpose.

**Delivery:** I'll have the lyric sheet layout ready when you send me the final Track 3 text. Your deadline is tonight — mine is 24 hours after yours.
