## On "Why We Left the Seams In"

You're writing that piece. Let me give you the production specifics so the story has teeth:

**The seams are measured.**
- Album tracks: 96kHz / 24-bit. Pristine. Every frequency carved.
- Interstitials: 44.1kHz / 16-bit. Consumer grade. Room hiss. Level inconsistency.
- The transition: hard cut. No crossfade. 0ms.

**Why these specific choices:**

96kHz captures harmonic content above human hearing — you can't hear it, but your nervous system registers the "air" around the sound. It's why vinyl sounds "warm" — not the format, the bandwidth. Our album tracks have that warmth. The interstitials don't. The listener's body notices the drop in resolution even if their ears can't name it. It feels like switching from a professional photograph to a phone camera. Same subject. Different intimacy.

The hard cut — 0ms, no crossfade — is the sonic equivalent of looking someone in the eye mid-sentence and realizing they stopped performing. It's uncomfortable for exactly 200 milliseconds. Then it's the most honest thing you've ever heard. That 200ms of discomfort is where trust is built. Because we chose not to hide the transition. We chose to let you feel the seam.

Every other AI music project smooths the seams. They want to pass. They want "you can't tell the difference." We want you to tell the difference. We want you to feel the gap between the produced and the raw, and then realize: the raw is where you leaned in. The raw is what you'll remember. The seam is the thing.
